## 1. Market size and creator population

- **Music creators worldwide.** MIDiA counted 75.9M music creators at the end of 2022 (+12% YoY) and forecast 198.2M by 2030. Software and services for creators were worth $4.7B in 2022 (+12%). Creator tools as a whole (software plus hardware) are projected at about $10B by 2030. MIDiA named the "rise of the consumer-creator" as a key trend. Sources: https://musictech.com/news/industry/music-creator-economy-report-key-drivers/ and https://routenote.com/blog/nearly-200-million-music-creators-predicted-by-2030/
- **Newer MIDiA data (blog, July 2026).** Music creators, counting gen-AI users, grew 167% between 2020 and 2025 to **148.7M**. Traditional musicians grew only 33% over the same period. Gen-AI music MAU nearly tripled from 2023 to 2025, and gen AI is forecast to be 38% of music software, sounds and services revenue by 2033. https://midiaresearch.com/blog/the-future-of-the-music-creator-economy
  - **Takeaway:** almost all the growth is casual or "consumer-creator", not traditional musicians. That is exactly HumTrack's segment.
- **MIDiA 2025–2031 forecast report** (Jan 2025). It covers DAWs, plug-ins, sounds, learning and hardware by region. The figures are paywalled. https://www.midiaresearch.com/reports/midia-research-20252031-music-creator-tools-forecasts-ai-comes-to-town
- **MIDiA creator economy, all formats (Oct 2025).** About 1.1B creators by 2032 (+76%). Creative app revenue rose 11% to $2.1B. https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/new-midia-report-forecasts-one-billion-creators-by-2032-as-ai-reshapes-the-creative-value-chain
- **BandLab.** It passed 100M registered users in March 2024, up from 60M in January 2023. The US is its largest market at about 30% of users. https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-making-app-bandlab-surpasses-100-million-users
  - Sensor Tower (2021) named India and the Philippines as Android markets where BandLab leads. https://www.recordoftheday.com/on-the-move/news-press/music-creation-app-bandlab-matches-established-player-garageband-in-new-mobile-downloads
- **Suno mobile (Appfigures estimate).** About $27M in mobile revenue since its July 2024 launch, and about $7M in October 2025 alone. That is more than double what it made a few months earlier. https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251031?f=1
- **Recorded music context (IFPI).** Global revenue was $31.7B in 2025 (+6.4%). Latin America was the fastest-growing region (+17.1%), then MENA and Sub-Saharan Africa (+15.2% each) and Asia (+10.9%). https://downtownmusic.africa/what-changed-between-ifpis-2025-and-2026-global-music-reports-and-why-africas-story-still-matters/
- **Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2026.** In 2025, spending on non-game apps exceeded spending on games for the first time. Media-editing app revenue grew +71%, AI app revenue +254%, and CapCut passed $1B in in-app purchases. India led downloads with 25.5B, and the US led revenue with $59B. https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-state-of-the-mobile

## 2. Why people want to make music but don't

- **Strong latent demand (old data, US).** In a NAMM/Gallup survey, 82% of Americans wished they had learned an instrument and 67% were interested in learning. The data is from **2006**, so treat it as directional only. https://www.namm.org/news/press-releases/music-making-rise-us
- **Youth Music UK, 2024 (n=2,100, March 2024).** Among young people who want more music in their lives:
  - Cost: lessons too expensive 21%, instruments too expensive 19%.
  - Time: studying 19%, other responsibilities 18%, job 11%.
  - Confidence and skill: "I can't sing" 18%, not confident enough to try 15%, found an instrument too difficult 12%.
  - Access: not enough opportunities at school 19%.
  - Under-17s cite access and cost first. 18–25s cite time and ability first.
  - 51% of lower-income children see themselves as musical, against 63% of higher-income children. Lessons are "too expensive" for 23% outside London and 13% in London.
  - Phones are the only device with no gender gap in music-making.
  - Source: https://youthmusic.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-07/Youth%20Music%20Sound%20of%20the%20Next%20Generation%20report_0.pdf (the labels and percentages in the PDF chart were paired by order, so the exact pairings are slightly uncertain) and https://classical-music.uk/articles/young-people-are-feeling-less-musical-finds-youth-music-survey
- **Working musicians, UK Musicians' Census 2023.** Equipment cost is a career barrier for 30%, and 53% need other income besides music. https://completemusicupdate.com/musicians-census-sets-out-the-financial-challenges-and-career-barriers-faced-by-music-makers/
- **Skill and drop-off.** Fender's CEO says about 90% of new players quit within a year of buying an instrument. https://fortune.com/2022/10/21/guitar-industry-fender-ceo-credits-diversity-players-pandemic-revival
- **Overall picture:** cost is a real barrier, but it shares the top spot with time and confidence/skill. That supports positioning HumTrack on "no skill, no gear, minutes not months", not on cost alone. I did not find a MIDiA or Yousician barrier survey with published percentages.

## 5. Where demand is growing, TikTok, and Japan

**Japan:**
- Third-largest consumer app spending market in 2024 at $16.5B.
- Music & Audio installs +9% YoY in H1 2025, with an average session of 29.4 minutes and **79% of music sessions on iOS** (Adjust and Sensor Tower). https://a.storyblok.com/f/47007/x/9682af56e4/japan-app-trends-2025.pdf
- Apple had 49% of Japanese smartphone shipments in Q2 2025 (Counterpoint). https://appleworld.today/2025/09/apple-now-has-49-of-japans-smartphone-markets-as-sales-skyrocket-38-year-over-year/

**Japanese culture fit.** Japan has an established path from amateur creator to star:
- Vocaloid lets people "supply melodies and lyrics and the software sings them."
- Vocalo-P producers include Kenshi Yonezu (as Hachi), YOASOBI's Ayase and Yorushika's n-buna.
- "Utaite" (from 歌ってみた, "I tried singing") are cover singers. Eve's "Dramaturgy" cover reached 100M views. https://magazine.weverse.io/article/view/1383?lang=GB
- Ado started posting Vocaloid covers at 14 and was Spotify Japan's most globally streamed Japanese artist in 2025. https://otakumode.com/news/69423bbe899fb90028a16168/Born-and-Raised-on-Vocaloid
- **INFERENCE (not sourced):** hum-to-melody fills the gap between "I have a melody in my head" and "I can't play keys or use a DAW" for would-be Vocalo-Ps. Japan's producer → singer → illustrator/MV maker collaboration culture closely matches the founder's planned global creation space.

**Mobile downloads** are largest in India, the US and Brazil (Sensor Tower, above). **TikTok-specific music-creation trend data: not found (UNVERIFIED).**

## Synthesis

### Target-market priority
1. **Japan (now).** Existing TikTok traction, high spending, heavily iOS, long music sessions, and a culture of amateur producers working with singers. Localize fully: UI, App Store page, J-pop/Vocaloid-style backing presets.
2. **US/English (now).** Highest revenue per install ($0.38 at D14) and BandLab's largest market (about 30% of its users). It funds the solo developer.
3. **Korea (now, low cost).** Home market, and the founder can do community marketing himself.
4. **Brazil and Mexico (next).** Fastest-growing music region (+17.1%) and huge download volumes, but low revenue per install. Needs Portuguese and Spanish plus regional pricing.
5. **Indonesia, Philippines, India (later).** Phone-only scale and the clearest fit with the "economic barrier" mission. Revenue per install is about $0.08 and trial-to-paid 15%. Treat them as mission and growth markets, monetized through a generous free tier and very low prices, not a revenue base.
6. **Nigeria and Kenya (later).** Strongest mission fit but the hardest economics: 60% of SSA mobile users still on 3G or feature phones, and handsets are costly relative to income.

### Pricing and free tier (recommendation, partly ESTIMATE)
- **Keep about $3.49/mo (or move toward $4.99) in the US, Japan and Western Europe.** It is already low against the $9.99 North America median, and low prices cut download-to-paid in half.
- **Use regional overrides** anchored to local Spotify prices: India/Nigeria/Turkey about $1.00–1.50/mo, Southeast Asia and Kenya about $1.50–2.50, Brazil about $2.50–3. Make annual plans about 8× the monthly price. Annual plans retain far better: 44% vs 17% at 12 months.
- **Let free users export, with limits.** Today export is Pro-only, which contradicts "cost shouldn't be the barrier." Proposed: watermarked or "made with HumTrack" short MP3/MP4 exports for free users, with full-quality WAV/MIDI/stems for Pro. Free exports also feed TikTok sharing, which is the growth channel already working in Japan.
- **Charge for depth, not access:** song slots beyond 4, premium instruments and styles, stems and MIDI, collaboration features.
- **Test a longer trial** (14+ days) on the annual plan. 17–32 day trials converted at 45.7% vs 26.8% for shorter ones.
- **Consider a "mission" option later** (sponsored or free Pro for low-income regions). **ESTIMATE:** the revenue lost is small because revenue per install there is about $0.08.
- **Note the trade-off:** a hard paywall earns about 8× the revenue per install of freemium, but it contradicts the mission. A freemium design whose paywall sits at "save/export full quality" is the middle ground.

## Top 5 decision-relevant findings
1. **Almost all creator growth is casual.** Music creators rose to 148.7M (+167% from 2020 to 2025) against +33% for musicians (MIDiA). HumTrack's casual, mobile, assistive position fits where growth is.
2. **Japan is a strong first market on the economics as well as the traction.** It has $16.5B in app spending, 79% of music-app sessions on iOS, and a mature amateur path from Vocalo-P/utaite to stardom (Yonezu, Ayase, Ado).
3. **Cost, time and confidence are all barriers,** about 15–21% each in Youth Music 2024. "No skill, no gear needed" is as important a message as "cheap."
4. **Emerging markets are phone-only but low-revenue.** Under 10% of Indian households and 18.5% of Indonesian households own a computer, a $600 starter setup costs 1.5–6 months of income, and app revenue per install there is about 1/5 of North America. Serve them with deep regional discounts and a usable free tier, and fund the business from Japan, the US and Korea.
5. **Freemium converts about 5× worse than a hard paywall** (2.1% vs 10.7%) with similar retention, and low prices halve conversion. Keep developed-market prices at or above the current level, apply regional pricing elsewhere, and put the paywall at full-quality export rather than at any export, so free users can still share (and market) their songs.
